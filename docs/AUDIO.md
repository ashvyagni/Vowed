# VOWED — Audio

**Status:** Combat SFX implemented (M1). Adaptive music `[PLANNED M3]`.
Describes what exists; unbuilt systems are marked `[PLANNED]`.

---

## 1. Why audio is a combat system, not decoration

The master directive is explicit: *the player should be able to understand
successful actions partly through sound.*

In practice audio is the **fastest feedback channel a player has** — quicker to
register than a visual flash, and it works while the player's eyes are on the
opponent rather than on their own character. It is how the difference between
**hitting**, **being blocked**, and **whiffing** becomes legible without any UI.

A hit with no sound reads as a hit that did not land, however good the animation
is. That is why this was built in M1 alongside hitstop and impact VFX, rather
than deferred to a polish pass: the three together *are* the feel of a hit, and
tuning any of them without the others gives a misleading result.

## 2. Bus layout

```
Master
├── SFX        combat impacts, footsteps, world
├── Music      adaptive music layers          [PLANNED M3]
├── Dialogue   voice                          [PLANNED M6]
└── UI         menu and HUD feedback
```

Generated by `tools/generate_audio_buses.gd` into `default_bus_layout.tres`.

All four exist now even though two are unused. Retrofitting buses is painful —
every existing `AudioStreamPlayer` has to be revisited, and the settings menu's
volume sliders have nothing to bind to until the buses exist. **Dialogue is
separate from SFX specifically for accessibility**: dialogue volume must be
independently adjustable (`docs/MILESTONES.md` M16), and that is impossible if
it shares a bus.

## 3. Combat SFX

`src/audio/CombatAudio.gd`, autoload `CombatAudio`. Listens to `GameEvents` and
never touches combat — combat resolves a hit and is finished; this is one of the
observers that reacts (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §2.1).

| Event | Sound | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Clean hit (light) | `impactPunch_medium` | The baseline connect |
| Clean hit (heavy / launch / knockdown) | `impactPunch_heavy` **+** `impactSoft_heavy` | Weight is *layered*, not just louder |
| Guarded | `impactMetal_light` | Bright, metallic — unmistakably **not** a clean hit |
| **Perfect parry** | `impactBell_heavy` | The most distinctive sound in the game |
| Dodged | `impactGeneric_light` (quiet) | A near-miss should be felt, not announced |
| Stagger / defeat | `impactSoft_heavy` | Body, not strike |

### 3.1 The two rules that matter more than the samples

**Variation.** A combo fires this several times per second. One sample repeated
is *worse than silence* — it turns a flurry into a machine-gun artefact. Every
category has 5 variants, chosen at random, with pitch jittered ±11% on top, so
no two hits in a string are identical.

**Distinctness.** Hit, guard and parry use different *materials* — flesh, plate,
bell — rather than the same sample at different volumes. Volume differences do
not survive a busy mix; timbre differences do.

### 3.2 Implementation notes

- **16 pooled `AudioStreamPlayer3D` voices**, round-robin. Reusing the oldest
  means a long combo degrades by dropping its *oldest* impact rather than
  refusing to play the newest — and the newest is the one the player is
  listening for.
- **Positional**, with deliberately gentle falloff. Realistic attenuation would
  make a fight two metres away inaudible, costing the player information they
  need.
- **Sets are discovered, not listed.** Files are grouped by filename prefix at
  startup, so dropping in another variant puts it in rotation without a code
  change — content should never require one.

## 4. Verification

The integration suite asserts audio is wired
(`tests/integration/CombatPipeline.tscn`): sound sets load, and the four
categories a player must tell apart by ear are present.

Worth a test because **silence is invisible**. Every other system can be green
while combat makes no sound at all, and nobody notices until someone plays it
with the volume up. The specific failures it catches are an empty or mis-named
sound directory, and events never reaching the audio service.
